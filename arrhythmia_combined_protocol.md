# American Clinical Protocol for Heart Arrhythmia
*Reference Standards: ACC/AHA/HRS Guidelines*

This protocol outlines the clinical management strategies for common cardiac arrhythmias including Atrial Fibrillation (AFib), Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT), and Premature Ventricular Complexes (PVCs) across acute and long-term settings.

---

## 1. Atrial Fibrillation (AFib) Protocol

### Acute & Emergency Care
*   **Hemodynamically Unstable:** Immediate **synchronized cardioversion** is indicated regardless of AFib duration.
*   **Hemodynamically Stable:** 
    *   **Rate Control:** First-line intervention using intravenous **beta-blockers** (e.g., metoprolol, esmolol) or **nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers** (e.g., diltiazem, verapamil).
    *   **Rhythm Control:** Chemical cardioversion (e.g., ibutilide, amiodarone) or electrical cardioversion can be considered if the patient is symptomatic and the thrombus risk is ruled out (AFib duration <48 hours or confirmed absence of thrombus via transesophageal echocardiogram).
*   **Thromboembolic Assessment:** Evaluate acute stroke risk and initiate heparin or direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) as indicated.

### Long-Term Management
*   **Stroke Risk Stratification:** Utilise the **CHA2DS2-VASc score** to determine individual risk.
*   **Anticoagulation Therapy:** **DOACs** (e.g., apixaban, rivaroxaban) are preferred over warfarin for non-valvular AFib. Consider **left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) devices** for patients with high stroke risk and absolute contraindications to long-term anticoagulation.
*   **Rate vs. Rhythm Control Strategy:**
    *   **Chronic Rate Control:** Oral beta-blockers, diltiazem, or verapamil targeting a resting heart rate <110 bpm in asymptomatic patients.
    *   **Chronic Rhythm Control:** Antiarrhythmic drugs (AADs) such as amiodarone, sotalol, flecainide, or propafenone.
    *   **Catheter Ablation:** Elevated to a **Class 1 recommendation** for symptomatic paroxysmal AFib, and preferred over AADs in selected patients or those with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction.
*   **Lifestyle & Risk Factor Modification:** Aggressive management of weight, sleep apnea, hypertension, diabetes, and cessation of alcohol and smoking.

## 2. Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT) Protocol

### Acute & Emergency Care
*   **Hemodynamically Unstable:** Immediate **synchronized cardioversion**.
*   **Hemodynamically Stable:**
    *   **Vagal Maneuvers:** Perform a **modified Valsalva maneuver** or carotid sinus massage as the first-line physical intervention.
    *   **Pharmacological Therapy:** Administer **intravenous adenosine** (rapid bolus: 6 mg, followed by 12 mg if unsuccessful) under continuous ECG monitoring.
    *   **Second-Line Therapy:** IV beta-blockers or IV nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers if adenosine fails to terminate the rhythm.

### Long-Term Management
*   **Pharmacological Prevention:** Oral beta-blockers, diltiazem, or verapamil are used as first-line maintenance therapy for recurrent symptomatic episodes. Class Ic drugs (flecainide, propafenone) may be used in patients without structural heart disease.
*   **Catheter Ablation:** Highly recommended (**Class 1**) as a definitive, curative strategy for recurrent symptomatic SVT (e.g., AVNRT, AVRT, focal atrial tachycardia).

## 3. Premature Ventricular Complexes (PVCs) Protocol

### Acute & Emergency Care
*   **Asymptomatic PVCs:** Do not require acute treatment, regardless of frequency.
*   **Symptomatic or Frequent PVCs (Acute Settling):**
    *   **Electrolyte Optimization:** Identify and correct electrolyte derangements immediately; maintain serum **potassium >4.0 mEq/L** and **magnesium >2.0 mEq/L**.
    *   **Pharmacological Suppression:** Intravenous **beta-blockers** can be administered if symptoms are distressing or if PVCs are triggering malignant ventricular arrhythmias (e.g., R-on-T phenomenon causing VT/VF).

### Long-Term Management
*   **Diagnostic Burden Assessment:** Quantify PVC burden using a **24-to-48-hour Holter monitor**. Evaluation for structural heart disease via transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE).
*   **Threshold for Intervention:** Treatment is indicated for highly symptomatic patients or a high PVC burden (typically **>10–15% of total beats**) due to the risk of PVC-induced cardiomyopathy.
*   **First-Line Medical Therapy:** Oral **beta-blockers** or **nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers**.
*   **Second-Line Medical Therapy:** Antiarrhythmic drugs (e.g., flecainide or propafenone in patients without structural heart disease; amiodarone or sotalol in patients with structural heart disease).
*   **Catheter Ablation:** Indicated for symptomatic frequent PVCs when medical therapy fails or as an initial strategy for patients with PVC-induced cardiomyopathy to restore ventricular function.
